Decision Arena

System One vs small LLMs, side by side

On the board Jev 1.13 · GPT-5.6 Luna · Claude Haiku 4.5 · Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite · rule-based bots

Every game gives each controller its own copy of the same world, from the same seed. They read the same text and answer the same typed questions. Then you watch who decides well, who decides fast, and what it costs.

How a match works

Same world
A seed generates the traffic, calls, parcels or road. Every controller gets its own identical copy.
Same questions
Jev receives typed questions natively. LLMs get the same options and wording as a strict JSON schema.
Two clocks
Turn based pauses the world while a controller thinks. Real time keeps it moving, so slow answers land late.
Everything logged
Each decision's latency, tokens, cost and answers can be downloaded as JSON after every match.

What others have found so far

  • Speed and price hold up in every independent test: 0.2–0.7 s and a fraction of a cent per call.
  • From a raw chess board Jev plays worse than random; given code-computed facts it plays around 950 Elo. wondertwins/jev-benchmark
  • One big choice beat a two-step choice (piece, then square) in the same chess tests.
  • Nobody has published a reliability curve for Jev yet. City Dispatch draws one live.
  • The founder agreed it is "basically a zero-shot classifier". Hacker News

First pass in this arena (pilot, few seeds)

  • Sorting Hub: Jev routed every parcel right when extracting or walking the tree. With reasoning off, Luna and Haiku read pounds as kilograms.
  • Choosing among 125 bays at once: Jev 21/24, Luna and Haiku 10/24. Above 255 bays Jev cannot answer at all.
  • Night Highway: code-computed facts took Jev from 5 crashes to 0. In real time the LLMs crashed 10–13 times (with a rate-limit caveat).
  • City Dispatch: no model fell for a prank or an injected order. Level 4 is too easy to separate them.
  • Tower Control: facts removed the errors they describe, but nobody learned to sequence arrivals. A 40-line bot still wins.
